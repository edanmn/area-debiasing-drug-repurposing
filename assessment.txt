Overall assessment
My recommendation is to revise the paper substantially before submitting it to RECOMB 2027. The research question is interesting, the experimental results contain a potentially valuable methodological insight, and the paper demonstrates greater scientific transparency than many early-stage manuscripts.
However, I see several issues that could lead to rejection, particularly concerning the central theoretical claim, the interpretation of the alignment metric, and the strength of the experimental validation.
The paper's biggest opportunity is to establish a generalizable method for determining whether evaluation metrics meaningfully assess different debiasing mechanisms. That is potentially a better RECOMB contribution than the negative-control drug-repurposing method itself.

What the paper does well
1. It identifies a genuine methodological concern. A bias metric may favor a correction specifically designed around the same quantities that the metric measures.
2. It reports unfavorable results transparently. Table 2 shows that naive subtraction generally outperforms the proposed negative-control correction on the reported bias diagnostics. Rather than concealing this, the paper investigates why.
3. It tests multiple explanations. Negative-control validity, shrinkage, identifiability, and penalty-term alignment are examined instead of relying on a single experiment.
4. It contains a potentially useful general contribution. The alignment check could extend beyond drug repurposing to computational biology methods that remove confounding through residualization.
5. It makes some effort toward cross-domain validation. The genomics and bladderbatch experiments broaden the investigation beyond its original drug-repurposing application.
The strongest numerical observation is in Table 3: naive subtraction's penalty terms achieve AUROC values of approximately 0.60–0.79 against the selected flags, while the proposed correction's terms remain near 0.50. This is a meaningful finding worth examining further.
Major weaknesses that reviewers may identify
Critical
1. The central interpretation of AUROC is too strong

Sections 4.1, 5.7, and Abstract
The paper argues that when a correction's penalty term achieves approximately 0.50 AUROC against a bias flag, the metric cannot detect that correction's mechanism.
This conclusion is not generally justified.
An AUROC of 0.50 means that the penalty term does not discriminate between flagged and unflagged compounds in the measured comparison. It does not necessarily imply that subtracting that term cannot alter a top-50 ranking metric.
Ranking changes depend on the relationships among the base score, penalty term, and flag. A penalty may influence which compounds enter the top 50 even when its standalone AUROC is near 0.50.
Required improvement: Introduce an intervention-based test. Scale or perturb each correction's penalty while holding the baseline score fixed, and directly measure how each bias metric responds.
This would make the alignment check substantially more convincing.

Critical
2. The theoretical argument does not yet establish the proposed general result

Section 4.1, pages 5–6
The mathematical result relies on the residual score equations of regularized logistic regression.
The authors correctly acknowledge that exact orthogonality does not hold under finite L2 regularization. Indeed, their measured projection of the factor onto the representation's column space is 34–40%.
That is a substantial deviation from exact orthogonality.
Moreover, even exact linear orthogonality between a factor and the representation does not guarantee independence from a nonlinear binary flag predicted using that representation.
The bladderbatch experiment is particularly important here: the flag is moderately predictable from the representation, yet the residualized factor remains informative about the flag.
Required improvement: Separate three distinct claims:
- Algebraic orthogonality of residuals.
- Statistical association between a penalty and a bias flag.
- Actual sensitivity of the evaluation metric to the correction.
Establish which implications hold mathematically, which are empirical, and which require additional assumptions.
I would avoid presenting the current two-step diagnostic as a general theoretical guarantee.

Critical
3. The proposed correction is insufficiently specified for unseen compounds

Section 4 and Section 5.2
The negative-control factor is constructed from left singular vectors of training residuals, producing sample-level factors.
The manuscript then describes cold-start-drug experiments, where test compounds are unseen during model fitting.
It is not clear how the factor values are generated for those compounds.
This matters because the proposed penalty is later evaluated on non-indicated test compounds.
Required improvement: Provide the exact out-of-sample factor-estimation procedure, including how the model constructs factors and counterfactual penalty terms for test drugs. Also specify which data are accessible during each cross-validation scheme.
Without this, reviewers cannot reliably assess the method's validity or reproducibility.

Major
4. The comparison does not establish superiority of the proposed method

Tables 1 and 2
The actual results show that the proposed method largely preserves plain-model AUPRC, but naive subtraction performs better on the stated bias metrics.
For example:
Epilepsy metric	Plain	Naive	Proposed
AUPRC	0.091	0.090	0.085
Same-area rate	0.144	0.068	0.176
Proconvulsant rank percentile	30.9	71.8	26.3
The proposed correction performs worse than naive subtraction on all three measures shown here.
Demonstrating metric misalignment does not establish that the proposed method removes biologically meaningful confounding more successfully.
Required improvement: Clearly distinguish a diagnostic contribution from a successful debiasing algorithm. Add independent evidence that the residualized factor captures unwanted variation, ideally using controlled synthetic experiments with known confounders.

Major
5. The validation dataset is limited

Sections 3, 5.2, and 6
The manuscript reports ATC classifications for only 9.7% of the library and SIDER coverage of only 5.5%.
This makes the bias diagnostics dependent on a small, potentially nonrepresentative subset of compounds.
Another concern is that the main comparative results cover three disease categories despite constructing an 18-indication dataset.
Required improvement: Evaluate additional indications, report flag prevalence and confidence intervals, and analyze whether the results change across adequately covered subsets. A separate benchmark would strengthen generalization claims.

Additional technical issues
These are less central than the five problems above, but they could attract reviewer scrutiny.
Issue	Recommendation
Shrinkage experiment (Section 5.5)	Scaling a factor before refitting may not amount to ordinary shrinkage; coefficients can compensate for scaling. Explain how regularization changes the result.
CVS3 validity	The paper acknowledges that target-specific methods collapse together. Consider removing CVS3 from claims of meaningful comparative validation.
Matrix-factorization leakage	Explicitly verify that the shared 16-dimensional embedding is fitted within each training fold and does not use held-out indication labels.
Negative-control validity	Small AUPRC changes do not prove mechanistic independence or identify an unbiased area factor. Narrow this conclusion.
Uncertainty reporting	Add paired effect sizes and confidence intervals for Table 2 and Table 3, not just Table 1.
External validation	The manuscript acknowledges skipping repoDB. If feasible, add this benchmark rather than leaving it as a limitation.
Related work	Complete the comparison with DRDM and strengthen the discussion of existing diagnostic and residualization methods.
One important writing issue is the use of phrases such as rules out noise, rules it out, and Table 3 is the resolution. The evidence often makes an explanation less likely rather than eliminating it. More qualified wording would better match the results.
What I would prioritize before submission
I would focus effort on the central methodological contribution rather than expanding every experiment equally.

Priority 1 — Establish that the alignment check predicts metric sensitivity
Run controlled penalty-intervention experiments. Show whether standalone AUROC actually predicts changes in the evaluated ranking metrics.
This is the single most important addition.




Priority 2 — Introduce synthetic data with known confounding
Build a simulation where disease-specific signal, organ-system confounding, and negative controls are independently adjustable.
Compare what each debiasing correction removes with what each metric detects. This would provide the ground truth missing from the real-drug experiments.




Priority 3 — Repair and sharpen the formal proposition
State precise assumptions and prove only the claims those assumptions support. Use the bladderbatch result as evidence of the limits of the current heuristic, rather than treating it as a peripheral complication.




Priority 4 — Audit the cross-validation implementation
Document the complete training and test pipeline, including matrix factorization, normalization, negative-control fitting, factor projection, and hyperparameter selection.




Priority 5 — Reframe the abstract and conclusions
Present the paper as a study of evaluation-metric sensitivity, not as proof that an unsuccessful correction is actually superior or that current metrics cannot detect it under all circumstances.

Final verdict
I would not abandon this research idea. The most promising part of the paper is the observation that a debiasing benchmark's outcome can depend on how closely its measurement target matches a correction's mechanism.
But there is an important difference between showing that two penalty terms have different associations with bias flags and proving that an evaluation metric is incapable of detecting one correction's effect.
That distinction is currently the central vulnerability.
If you can demonstrate the alignment check's practical validity using controlled experiments, clarify the theory, and resolve the out-of-sample methodology, I would regard this as a materially stronger RECOMB submission.
My final recommendation: Major revision, with the alignment-check theory and empirical validation taking precedence over additional drug-repurposing benchmarks.
